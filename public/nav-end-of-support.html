What end of support actually means

What stops

Microsoft’s work on that version

  • Security updates and hotfixes for NAV itself
  • Tax and regulatory updates from Microsoft
  • Microsoft support cases for that version
  • Often, your partner’s appetite for supporting it
What doesn’t

Everything your business runs on

  • NAV keeps posting, invoicing and closing the books
  • Your perpetual licence stays valid — it never expires
  • Your data, customisations and integrations stay put
  • Your right to choose when, or whether, to upgrade
End of support is a risk to manage, not a deadline to panic about.

The real exposure is security: an unpatched application on an unpatched server, reachable from places it shouldn’t be. That’s fixable without replacing your ERP — supported Windows and SQL underneath, no direct internet exposure, tight accounts and permissions, and reliable backups.

Questions we get asked

Will NAV stop working when support ends?

No. NAV is perpetually licensed software installed on your own servers. It keeps running exactly as it does today. What stops is Microsoft’s security updates, hotfixes, regulatory updates and support for that version.

Is it still legal and licensed to run NAV after end of support?

Yes. A perpetual licence doesn’t expire when Microsoft’s support lifecycle ends. You keep the right to use the version you licensed.

Do we have to migrate to Business Central?

No. Migration is one option, not an obligation. Many businesses run older NAV versions safely for years by keeping the surrounding infrastructure patched and limiting exposure. The right choice depends on your version, customisations, infrastructure and plans.

What about the Windows Server and SQL Server NAV runs on?

Those have their own lifecycles. Windows Server 2016 reaches end of support on 12 January 2027 and SQL Server 2017 on 12 October 2027. Moving NAV onto a supported server and database is often a bigger security win than the NAV version itself.
